Key concept

Immersion: the variable that matters most

The shroud is the duct between the cooler package and the fan. It has three segments: a straight Box (P1), a narrowing Transition (P2) and a cylindrical Ring (P3) that wraps around the fan blades.

is the share of the blade's axial depth that sits inside the ring. At 0 the blade is completely outside, at 1 the ring covers its whole depth, above 1 the ring reaches past it. Lengthening any segment pushes the shroud front further over the fan, so immersion is calculated from the depths rather than measured.

steel Imm = (77.8 + Ring + Trans) / 103.8
plastic Imm = 1 − (41.522 − (P1 + P2 + P3)) / 76
all depths in mm; fan depth 103.8 mm (steel), 76 mm (plastic)

Why it matters. With too little ring around the blades, air leaks round the blade tips from the pressure side back to the suction side. With too much, the ring covers the part of the blade where air leaves with an outward component and chokes the outflow. In between lies a sweet spot.

Both shrouds in this data deliver the most air near immersion 0.55–0.6, consistent with the common rule of thumb of about half to two-thirds immersion. The steel baseline sits at 0.75, past that peak. The explanation above is the usual one in fan engineering; the engineers who ran the tests should confirm it for this machine.

Same immersion, different shape. Immersion says how far the shroud covers the fan, not how that cover is built. Near 0.6, steel shrouds with a long ring and a shortened transition deliver roughly 10–15% less air than balanced ones. That is why the framework adds a second feature, the split (Ring − Transition).

Correlation and mutual information

STEP 2 · NOTEBOOK §3

Find the structure

How many independent things are going on? Unsupervised methods answer this without a target.

Core

PCA of the outputs

finds how many independent patterns hide in the outputs.

On this dataSteel: one pattern explains 94% of the variation in all six outputs. Plastic needs about three (62 / 20 / 14%).

How to read it

One dominant pattern means "more air through everything", and one model is nearly enough. Several patterns mean the split between coolers changes on its own and needs its own model.

§3 · structure()
Core

Input correlation check

PCA and correlations of the inputs check the test plan itself.

On this dataSteel Ring and Transition depths correlate at r = −0.60, a side effect of optimiser-driven runs.

How to read it

When two inputs always change together, no model can tell which one caused an effect. That is a property of the data, and better test planning fixes it.

§3 · structure()
Core

Flow-split fractions

Each cooler's share of the total air flow, plotted against the design variables.

On this dataSteel: the charge-air cooler's share drops from 17% to 3.5% at high immersion. Plastic: total flow moves 13%, CAC flow twofold.

How to read it

A design can raise total flow and still starve one cooler. Fractions make that visible and become a constraint in the optimisation step.

§3 · structure()
  • OptionalClustering (k-means, Gaussian mixtures). Offers named operating regimes. Why not core: with 29 runs a small decision tree on immersion finds the same thresholds and is easier to explain.
  • Not nowt-SNE and UMAP. Offer 2-D maps of many-dimensional data. Why not: the inputs have only 2–4 dimensions and can be plotted directly.
STEP 3 · NOTEBOOK §4

Build physics features

Which variables should the model see? Physics-based inputs beat raw ones when data is scarce.

Core

Physics coordinates

Describe the shroud by immersion (how far it covers the fan) and split (Ring − Transition, how that cover is built) instead of raw segment depths.

On this dataPredicting later steel runs: error 0.15 → 0.10 kg/s. Random forest 0.56 → 0.88.

How to read it

Immersion is the sum of the depths, so it can't sit next to all of them in one model. Switching to immersion + split removes that overlap and matches how the flow responds.

§4 · add_features()
Core

Total plus fractions

Model the total flow and each cooler's share instead of four separate flows.

On this dataGuarantees that predicted cooler flows add up to the predicted total.

How to read it

Four independent models can predict components that don't sum to the total, which is physically impossible. Shares are log-ratio transformed so they also stay between 0 and 1.

§4 · add_features()
Core

Baseline normalisation

Express each output relative to its shroud's baseline.

On this dataPuts both shrouds on one scale: both peak near immersion 0.55–0.6.

How to read it

Normalised values compare designs across shrouds. Full fan-law scaling needs fan speed and pressure rise, which the workbook doesn't contain yet.

§4 · add_features()
Core

Relevance-based selection

Drop inputs that carry no signal, judged by the Gaussian process's , Sobol indices and mutual information together.

On this dataSteel triangles show no detectable effect on flow or power in the tested range.

How to read it

Fewer inputs give more stable models when runs are few. "No detectable effect" can also mean the tested range was too narrow, so ask the engineers before dropping a parameter for good.

§4, §7 · explain()
  • Not nowFan-law coefficients (flow, pressure, power). Offer pooling across fans, speeds and machines. Why not: needs fan speed and pressure rise for every run.
STEP 4 · NOTEBOOK §5

Choose models

Which model fits this kind of data? Start simple; add flexibility only when validation pays for it.

Core

Quadratic response surface

A smooth second-order formula in the inputs, the classic of designed experiments. Ridge regularisation and AIC/BIC keep only the terms the data supports.

On this dataBest on plastic: Q² 0.92 for total flow, 0.96 for CAC flow.

How to read it

Coefficients read directly as effects and interactions. Its fixed bowl shape can't follow a plateau, peak and drop, which is why it loses on steel.

§5 · model_zoo()
Core

Gaussian process (Kriging)

A flexible, smooth interpolator that returns an uncertainty with every prediction and learns how relevant each input is.

On this dataBest on steel: Q² 0.98 for total flow (error 0.06 kg/s), 0.96 for fan power.

How to read it

Its uncertainty drives the next-run recommender. With few runs the fit is fragile (it failed on plastic with default settings), so the framework uses several restarts, sensible bounds and a trend term. Outside the data it falls back to the average; never trust it there.

§5 · model_zoo()
Core

Linear model

A straight-line fit, kept as the reference every other model has to beat.

On this dataSteel total flow Q² 0.67; plastic total flow below 0.

How to read it

If a complex model barely beats the linear one, the extra complexity isn't paying off.

§5 · model_zoo()

No single model wins: it depends on the dataset

Leave-one-out ; best model per output in amber. Physics features: immersion + split (steel), immersion + Box + Ring (plastic). Plastic total uses Run 17 as recorded.

  • OptionalRandom forest. Offers a robust fit with no tuning. Why only as a contrast: step-shaped predictions, no extrapolation, weak below about 30 runs.
  • OptionalSymbolic regression. Offers a closed-form formula engineers can use in a spreadsheet. Why optional: unstable with little data; a bonus check, not the main model.
  • Not nowNeural networks and physics-informed networks. Offer very flexible fits. Why not: they overfit 29 runs; viable with hundreds of CFD runs.
  • Not nowCombining CFD and tests (multi-fidelity co-kriging). Offers many cheap simulations corrected by a few tests. Why not: needs runs labelled by source from both.
STEP 5 · NOTEBOOK §6

Validate honestly

How good are the predictions on designs the model has never seen?

Core

Leave-one-out and nested cross-validation

Each run is predicted by a model trained on all the others (). means the model choice is also made inside the loop.

On this dataAll Q² values in this guide come from leave-one-out. Without nesting they are slightly optimistic.

How to read it

With 7–29 runs there is no room for a separate test set; leave-one-out uses every run for testing once. Nesting stops the score from flattering whichever model happened to win.

§6 · cross_validate()
Core

Later-runs test

Train on the earlier runs and predict the later ones, the way the model will be used in practice.

On this dataSteel Runs 21–29 predicted with 0.10 kg/s ; plastic Runs 16–21 within about 1%, except Run 17.

How to read it

Later runs often explore new regions. This test shows how the model copes with designs unlike its training data.

§6 · cross_validate()
Core

Parity and residual plots

Predicted vs measured values, and the errors plotted against each input.

On this dataPlastic Run 17 stands out as the largest error (0.073 kg/s); its component sum fits the model.

How to read it

A pattern in the errors means missing physics. A single large error often means a data problem rather than a model problem.

§6 · diagnostics()
Core

Uncertainty check

Count how many measured runs fall inside the model's 95% bands.

On this dataThe steel baseline, never used in training, is predicted at 2.22 ± 0.03 kg/s; measured 2.239 kg/s.

How to read it

If far fewer than 95% of runs land inside the bands, the bands are too narrow and the next-run recommender will be overconfident.

Known issues in this dataset

Found by the automated checks. None blocks the analysis; each stays flagged in every output until the engineers who produced the data answer.

!

Run 17 total doesn't match its components Open

Plastic shroud. The recorded total is 3.6138 kg/s; the four components sum to 3.6739 kg/s. Probably a typo, not yet confirmed. The notebook keeps the recorded value and runs a corrected variant as a check.

!

Measurement, simulation, or both? Open

Plastic Runs 1–15 carry 13+ digits and add up exactly; Runs 0 and 16–21 are rounded. The data may mix simulation and measurement. Until this is clarified, the model learns the noise level from the data.

!

Runs chosen by an optimiser Handled

Inputs are correlated (steel Ring vs Transition r = −0.60) and steel has a gap at immersion 0.37–0.56. Physics features and the next-run recommender deal with both.

!

Parameters without definitions Open

LDR 700 "Parameter 1" has no name or unit. The steel triangle parameters and the steel baseline geometry are not specified. The questions are listed in the build proposal.

Garbage in, garbage out

Better data, better models

Model quality is capped by the data. These eight habits make the next test or simulation campaign ready for ML.

  1. Record the operating pointFan speed, air temperature and density, pressure rise and fan power for every run. Without them results can't be scaled to another fan or speed.
  2. Label the source of every numberTest bench or CFD, with rig or solver version and date. Mixed sources are fine; unlabelled ones are not.
  3. Plan in physics coordinatesSpread runs evenly over immersion and split (Latin hypercube or Sobol sequence). About 10 runs per input is a good start.
  4. Add runs in small batches3–5 at a time, chosen by the model where it is most uncertain or most promising.
  5. Repeat the baseline3–5 times across the campaign. Without it nobody can say whether a 0.5% difference is real.
  6. Pair optimiser runs with an even base setOptimiser runs alone produce correlated inputs and gaps, as in this data.
  7. Keep one row per runUnits in the header, no merged cells, totals and immersion calculated in code instead of typed.
  8. State the goal and the limitsMinimum flow per cooler and packaging bounds for each depth, so the optimiser proposes only feasible designs.
